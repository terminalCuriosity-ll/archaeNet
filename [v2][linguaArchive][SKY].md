# SKYSWIPED Lingua Archive

30 Sept, 2026 · @IOD::PAS-0264-72-CBRS-UU:Dragonfly

*Non-Diegetic notes.*

Sixteen languages of the Universal Authority and the Cooperative, about 600 years on from their 2026 forms; fluency is each earlier rate raised by 20% (x1.2, rounded).

## Languages

| Language | Fluency | Script | Majority-spoken | 600-year lineage | Learner trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mandarin | 96% | Mandarin hanzi | Cooperative worlds (Dí, Hǎi, Mēréi Žùn) | Hanzi stayed; speech did not. Tones collapsed to two registers, so compounds pile up for clarity, and "you" split into seven caste forms. | Using a "you" below the listener's caste is an offense; the top form is the safe guess. |
| Ēnglǔ | 74% | Ēnglǔ (macron and caron Latin) | UA station-cities, Fos | English lost "th" (to f/v), most verb endings and the "sh" of its own name; a clipped "ǔ" (from "you") ends greetings and questions. Corporate brands became verbs. | Brand-verbs shift with licensing: "to Kasai" is to shoot, "to 1Media" is to broadcast. |
| Bhaasha | 72% | Hindi and Urdu scripts, side by side | Manprava, Naraka | Hindi and Urdu re-fused. Case words welded onto nouns (ko becomes -ku), aspirates sank into breathy vowels, Sanskrit and Persian doublets compounded ("shukdhan"). | Every idea has two words; choosing one side marks your circle, so mix them. |
| Nuv Spãl | 56% | Ēnglǔ | Nwvae Teñodziçen | Spanish fused with Nahuatl: ll and y merged, final -s aspirated away, and Nahuatl's -tl returned as a suffix marking owned property. | Dropping -tl from a corp-owned thing says nobody owns it, which is an accusation. |
| Aurba | 50% | Arabic | Kaagdiholhi | Spoken Arabic never re-merged with the formal register, which became liturgy. First-syllable vowels vanished and "the" moved behind the noun. | The article comes last ("kitab-l"); putting it first sounds like scripture. |
| Ooledoen | 46% | Korean (hangul) | Shayhao Doshi | Korean's speech levels collapsed from many to three; Mandarin and Ēnglǔ loans displaced Sino-Korean words; the name means "the old tongue" and speakers wear it proudly. | Politeness rides the last syllable; end a sentence bare and you have spoken as a superior. |
| Ataruhonjin | 31% | Japanese (kana and kanji, katakana for loans) | Varong Ain | Honorifics were replaced by corporate rank suffixes; pitch accent flattened; verb endings clipped (-masu became -s); Ryukyuan and Korean words merged in. | The wrong rank suffix promotes or demotes the listener a step. |
| Cantonese | 26% | Mandarin hanzi (with colloquial characters) | shi'Edho | Kept its stops and grew from six tones to nine to fit Ēnglǔ loans. Sentence-final particles stack into mood chords. | Particles outweigh verbs; drop them and a request becomes an order. |
| Bengali | 19% | Hindi script (Devanagari) | Shanu | Its own script was lost. Devanagari cannot write Bengali's /ɔ/, so o and ɔ merged; classifiers dropped; Bhaasha loans flooded in. | False friends with Bhaasha: same spelling, different pronunciation and meaning. |
| Kiswahili | 13% | Arabic (Ajami revived) | n-Angga Sudı | Returned to Arabic script. In the Floating City, noun classes regrouped into five by how a thing floats: light, heavy, liquid, living, idea. Verb chains clipped (ni-na-ku- to n'ku-). | Class follows buoyancy, not size: a ship is "light", an anchor "heavy", and the verb must agree. |
| Nyvo Tunge | 11% | Braille (8-dot) | Ødemark | Danish, Swedish and Norwegian merged. Deep-sea acoustics favored hard consonants; vowels shrank to five and Danish stød became a word-final glottal stop. | Skip the final stop (') and words run together, especially underwater. |
| Hausa | 8% | Tifinagh | Taopatoain | Tifinagh writes consonants only, so vowels went unwritten, then unmarked; vowel harmony spread; gender collapsed; Tamasheq and Aurba loans entered caravan speech. | Vowels are predicted, not written: guess the harmony and you may still miss the meaning. |
| Afrikaans | 7% | Ēnglǔ | New Prospect | Dutch, Malay, Khoi and Bantu blend. w became v, d became t, h dropped, final -e fell; "nie ... nie" became a triple "nie ... nie ... nie" for legal certainty. | Skip the third nie in a contract and the clause is left unfinished, not binding. |
| Ansee Arshe | 6% | Ēnglǔ | Raĝale (Crown) | Ark-ship French, kept by noble houses. Liaison consonants grew into initial mutations (gender and possession change a word's first sound); nasal vowels split into vowel plus n. | Mutation is compulsory: omit it and you read as a commoner, overdo it and you mock. |
| Russian | 4% | Russian (Cyrillic) | Gorod Rassveta | Cold-shortened: unstressed vowels vanished, six cases collapsed into one genitive-like case, verbs take short aspect prefixes (sh-, po-). | Consonant clusters strain outsiders; adding vowels sounds childish. |
| Bantu | 4% | Ge'ez (Ethiopic) | Navyumbi | A rebuilt Proto-Bantu interlanguage from Zulu, Shona, Lingala and others. No native speakers; Ge'ez syllables fit its consonant-vowel shape. | Class prefixes repeat across the whole sentence; miss one and agreement collapses. |
